# Market Size

**Placement:** Chapter 3 → Communicate Your Story → Market Size
**Length:** ~1:50 to 2:00

---

Market size answers one question. How big can this get?

The cleanest framework on this comes from Pear VC. We adopt it here in full because we agree with it. Five principles for sizing your market.

First. Skip TAM, SAM, SOM. The acronyms encourage top-down thinking. The market-share assumption at the end becomes an unsubstantiated afterthought, which leaves you with a number that doesn't mean much to investors.

Second. Build it bottom up. Multiply the number of customers by the average revenue per customer per year. For transactional businesses, use transaction volume times price. The assumptions get substantiated by the rest of your pitch. Bottom-up is the version investors can pressure-test.

Third. Show five plus years of future revenue. Data from a Series A VC suggests that future revenue matters more than current size, even at seed. A bottom-up build of revenue five-plus years out tends to land harder than a hypothetical percentage of TAM.

Fourth. Be realistic about market share. Pitch decks often claim a ten percent share. That assumption is anchored in the consumer FAANG world where dominant platforms exist. Most categories are more fragmented or under-penetrated. Pick a number you can justify with bottom-up math.

Fifth. Clarity over the number. Clarity of thought and enduring ambition matter more than the headline figure. The goal is to show you understand the market deeply, and you're targeting a big opportunity inside it. The number is downstream of that.

That's the framework. Pear's full guide goes deeper, and we've linked it in the playbook.

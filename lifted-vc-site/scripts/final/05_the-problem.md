# The Problem

**Placement:** Chapter 3 → Communicate Your Story → The Problem
**Length:** ~1:40 to 1:50

---

The problem is what every pitch is built around. Investors need to internalize it before the rest of the pitch can land.

Experienced fundraisers spend a disproportionate amount of time working on how they pitch the problem. It leads off in most collateral, and it's often the first subject of a meeting.

The instinct is to rush to the solution because that's the exciting part, but it's the problem that makes your solution interesting. Invest the time to make the investor see the problem through your eyes.

The best problem statements share the same four elements.

First. A specific scene. Anchor in a specific subject: a person, team, department, or company in a clear context. Specific scenes tend to land. Investors connect to something they can picture, then extrapolate to the market on their own.

Second. The customer's point of view. Tell the problem through their voice. Use their language, their texture, their experience. Even without quotes, staying in their frame helps investors see the problem more clearly. Also, if you have them, verbatim quotes help take it even deeper.

Third. The cost and scope. Show both the per-customer cost and how widespread the problem is. Hours burned, dollars lost, deals missed, customers churned. The number of subjects feeling the same pain, and what they collectively spend dealing with it. Also, establishing the scope here helps clarify your market size later in the pitch.

Fourth. The band-aid solutions. Walk through what customers do today to manage it. Hacky spreadsheets. Dedicated headcount. Third-party tools that almost work. Manual workarounds that break at scale. The silliness of those band-aids does double duty. It proves how badly customers want a fix, and it sets up the wedge for your solution.

Incorporate all four well, and you might even see the investor start predicting your solution before you've started pitching it.

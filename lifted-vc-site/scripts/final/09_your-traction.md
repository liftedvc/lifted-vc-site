# Your Traction

**Placement:** Chapter 3 → Communicate Your Story → Your Traction
**Length:** ~1:40 to 1:50

---

Investors anchor on traction more than almost anything else in your pitch. Which isn't surprising, because traction is an objective proof point.

The instinct for founders is to make traction sound as strong as possible, and do things like lump pilots, LOIs, and run rate into one "ARR" number, or stretch definitions to make the headline pop. But because this is a proof point for investors, the strongest move is to lead with clarity. A founder who claims six hundred thousand in ARR after annualizing one strong fifty thousand month loses credibility for the rest of the deal.

Use the right label for the right number. From strongest to weakest: actual paid revenue, signed bookings, marketplace GMV, non-binding LOIs. Each step down requires more context to land.

When traction is light, especially at pre-seed, investors are searching for conviction points: signals that the problem you believe is real is substantiated through the market itself. Lay out every conversation, every signal, every customer who moved you forward. Founder-market fit, customer-discovery depth, design partner commitments, and a working prototype all carry weight when the ARR number is light.

The accurate number with the correct label resonates more with investors.

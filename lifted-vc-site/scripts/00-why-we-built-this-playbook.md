There's no shortage of fundraising advice out there. Most of it is fragmented. Some of it is wrong. And almost none of it shows you how a real round actually gets raised, end to end.

That's why I built this playbook.

I founded and sold a YC company. I raised from top-tier funds. I spent years on the investor side. And over the last two years, I've coached more than a hundred founders through their pre-seed and seed rounds. They've raised over eighty million dollars. From Y Combinator, Andreessen Horowitz, Pear Ventures, Bessemer, Founder Collective, Hustle Fund, Techstars, and Khosla.

This playbook is the curriculum I wish I'd had on my own raises.

Eleven interactive chapters. Built from real raises, not theory. Every framework here came out of an actual coaching session with a real founder closing a real round. The patterns are the ones that showed up across hundreds of meetings, term sheets, and closes.

It's stage agnostic, pre-seed to Series A. Where the specifics differ by stage, like traction bars, check sizes, or what investors are evaluating, I call it out directly.

If you're raising, this is the curriculum. Hit Get the playbook below, and let's get to work.

## AI Voiceover Notes

- "YC" reads as "why see" in modern voices. If Golpo flattens it, expand the first instance to "Y Combinator" and leave the rest as YC. Currently the script uses "YC" once and "Y Combinator" once for variety, which AI voices handle well
- "Y Combinator, Andreessen Horowitz, Pear Ventures, Bessemer, Founder Collective, Hustle Fund, Techstars, and Khosla" is a long proper-noun string. Modern AI voices handle each one cleanly. The commas give natural cadence breaks
- "Eighty million dollars" written out instead of "$80M" so the voice reads it correctly
- "More than a hundred founders" written out instead of "100+" for the same reason
- Short fragment opener "Most of it is fragmented. Some of it is wrong." builds rhythm before the payoff line. Each gets a hard stop
- "Eleven interactive chapters. Built from real raises, not theory." is two short fragments back to back. Resist joining them with a comma
- "Hit Get the playbook below" — "Get the playbook" is the literal CTA button label on the landing page, so it reads naturally as a callout
- Closing "and let's get to work." should be read in one breath with the prior phrase
- Contractions kept throughout for conversational cadence

## Note on positioning

This is the conversion video on the landing page, before the email gate. Founders watch this to decide whether to start the playbook at all. The script leads with credibility (founded and sold a YC company, top-tier funds, hundreds of coached raises, named funds) because that's what earns the click on "Get the playbook."

This script also pairs with the "How to Use This Playbook" video that lives inside the playbook itself (sub-intro-0). That one is for founders who already opened it and want to know how to use it well. They're complementary, not duplicative.

# Script: The Ask

**Placement:** Chapter 3, Communicate Your Story → The Ask
**Target length:** ~0:40 to 0:50
**Status:** PARKED. Video skipped for now (low value). Placeholder removed from page. Script preserved here in case we revisit.

---

The ask is the moment a fundraising conversation turns from storytelling into a transaction.

One best practice: actually ask. Most founders dance around it, hoping the investor will offer unprompted. That offer rarely comes. The closing question is direct, specific, and forces a yes-or-no answer. Their reaction will give you a better sense on where you stand with them.

Here's a strong ask: "We're raising $2M on a SAFE at $15M post. $1.4M committed. Closing in three weeks. Are you in?"

Here's a weak one: "We're raising $2 to 3M at a fair valuation. Let me know if you're interested."

## AI Voiceover Notes

- "Founders who skip it leave deals on the table every meeting" — punchy stake-setter for the opener. A small beat after "table" lands the "every meeting" hammer
- "Most founders dance around the ask, hoping the investor will offer unprompted. They won't." — "They won't." is a hard two-word stop. Hold the beat after it
- The Move 01 example "We're raising $2M on a SAFE at $15M post. $1.4M committed. Closing in three weeks. Are you in?" — five short fragments inside the quote. Each period is a real stop. Read it like a founder actually saying it, not like a recitation
- "Investors fund the next valuation inflection, not runway or hiring plans" — the "not runway or hiring plans" is the contrast that makes the line work. Slight emphasis on "next valuation inflection"
- Three contrasting examples in Move 02: "go from $30K MRR to $150K MRR over 18 months" vs "$1.5M for 18 months of runway" vs "$1.5M to hire three engineers." First reads as the strong version, second and third read as weaker alternatives. Slight tonal lift on the first, flat on the others
- "Hiring is a means to an end. Lead with the outcome." — two short directives. Periods give the AI clear stops
- "18 to 24 months" should read as "eighteen to twenty-four months" — the AI handles this fine, but worth checking
- "Raising takes about six months; hitting milestones that make the next round fundable takes 12 to 18 of execution" — long-ish sentence with a semicolon. The AI may handle the semicolon as a comma; if the cadence drops, swap to a period
- The use-of-funds example "60% engineering to ship V2 and unlock enterprise pipeline. 25% go-to-market to reach $1M ARR. 15% reserve." — three short fragments, each a discrete budget line. Brief beats between them
- "is rarely the best outcome" — gentle contrarian beat. "Rarely" gets the lift
- "missing that bar leads to flat or down rounds" — three words at the end ("flat or down rounds") carry the stake. Slight emphasis on "down"
- Closer "did you actually ask, name the amount, tie it to a milestone, and request a decision?" — four-item list as a question. Each item is a checkpoint. Small beats between commas, rising inflection on "decision"
- Contractions kept throughout for conversational cadence

## Note on scoping

The page covers six moves and a strong/weak comparison; the script picks all six but condenses each. Drops the strong/weak comparison (lives on the page where it reads better as visual reference) and the surfaces tip chip (intro email, first call, partner meeting, follow-up, investor update, offline conversations).

The script also drops the deeper Kupor "tweener round" terminology from the page rebuild, since "tweener" is a16z-specific vocabulary that doesn't travel well outside that audience. The concept (the round that's too big for the milestones it'll fund) is implicit in the "size for the next valuation inflection" beat.

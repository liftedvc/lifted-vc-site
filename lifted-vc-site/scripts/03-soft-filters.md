# Script: Soft Filters

**Placement:** Section 1.3, The Filter System (second video)
**Target length:** ~2 minutes (under 2100 characters)

---

You've cut your list with hard filters. Now you tier what's left. Soft filters don't necessarily remove investors, but they do decide where you spend your effort. Tier one gets most of it. Tier two gets some. Tier three is backstop only.

Here are the five soft filters.

One. Lead appetite. If you've determined you need a lead investor, this filter makes sure you have enough leads in your pipeline. When evaluating an individual investor, if they've led at least ten percent of their deals, that's a bare minimum. Twenty percent would be strong. When evaluating your overall list, at least thirty percent of your tier one should be lead candidates.

Two. Check size. Most funds publicly state the check sizes they write. If they don't, a good rule of thumb is one to two percent of their total fund size. Either way, make sure your round size supports their check size.

Three. Valuation range. If you fall slightly outside their typical range, that's a yellow flag. If you're at twice their range, you've likely miscalculated stage fit, which is a hard filter.

Four. Market fit. Look at the markets their portfolio companies operate in. Enterprise software, consumer apps, developer tools, marketplaces. You want investors whose portfolio pattern matches the market you're building for.

Five. Traction expectations. Compare your traction to their recent investments at your stage. Some investors require revenue, others back pre-revenue companies. Know which type you're pitching.

Now the important framing. Your working list of eighty to one hundred twenty is tier ones and tier twos only. Tier threes don't count toward that number. They're backstop.

Here's an important note. Seventy percent of your working list should be tier ones. For example, if your list has one hundred eighty total investors, eighty tier ones, thirty of which are leads, thirty tier twos, and seventy tier threes. Your working list is one hundred ten. The tier threes might be the largest group on the spreadsheet, but they only get five percent of your effort.

Both sides of the table operate at volume. VCs see far more deals than they fund, and founders take far more meetings than they close.

VCs evaluate roughly one hundred and one opportunities for every deal they close. The average founder takes thirty-nine investor meetings to close a seed round.

Once the round closes, the math compounds. Equity is permanent until exit, and every priced round dilutes. Founders dilute roughly twenty percent per round. Median founder ownership lands around fifty-six percent after seed, thirty-six percent after Series A, and twenty-three percent after Series B.

What this means for founders.

Plan for the volume on the front end. Build a process that can handle dozens of meetings in a tight window. And weigh every round against the dilution it costs. Equity is the most expensive form of capital, and the bill compounds.

# Script: The Solution

**Placement:** Chapter 3, Communicate Your Story → The Solution (line ~5582 in fundraising-playbook.html)
**Target length:** ~1:45 to 1:55
**Status:** Placeholder added to page. Swap in YouTube ID after upload.

---

Communicating the solution well comes down to three questions.

First question. What is it? Two moves do the work here. Lead with the non-obvious insight. A thesis the market hasn't caught up to yet. For Stripe, it was "developers should be the buyer, not finance teams." Then describe the first product: what you're shipping today, in specific concrete terms. For Square, it was a credit card reader for tiny merchants.

Second question. How does it actually work? Show the mechanism, not the features. How the product works in one diagram, one paragraph, or one demo. For Uber, it was matching drivers and riders via GPS in real time. The mechanism is what makes the solution believable. When you can, show, don't just describe. A screenshot. A short loop of the killer interaction. A before-and-after that makes the gap visceral.

Third question. Why is this ten times better than the alternative? Pick where you beat the status quo. Faster, cheaper, simpler, more accessible, more delightful, or net new. Marginal improvements read as a vitamin, not a painkiller.

Here's the test. After hearing your solution explained, can the investor describe your company to a colleague in a few sentences? If yes, the explanation works.

## AI Voiceover Notes

- "First question. / Second question. / Third question." pattern is on purpose so each beat gets a hard stop. Resist reformatting to colons or commas
- "Stripe," "Uber," "Plaid," "Notion" all read as proper nouns, no special handling
- "ten times" written out instead of "10x" to avoid awkward TTS pronunciation
- "OAuth" pronounces as "oh-auth" cleanly in modern voices
- "Sixty seconds" written out so the voice reads it naturally
- "Before-and-after" hyphenated for cleaner compound-word reading
- The closing test ("After hearing your solution, can the listener describe your company to a colleague in one sentence? If yes, the explanation works.") has a small intonation lift on the question and a beat after "If yes" that lets the resolution land
- Contractions kept throughout for conversational cadence

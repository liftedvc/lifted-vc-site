# Script: Hard Filters

**Placement:** Section 1.3, The Filter System (first video)
**Target length:** ~2 minutes (under 2100 characters)

---

Every investor on your list goes through two passes. Hard filters come first. Fail one, and the investor comes off the list. No exceptions.

At first, founders find it difficult to embrace this, because they feel investors are a scarce resource. After you filter down, you'll find you have far more perfect-match investors than you thought possible.

An investor's decision is either a yes or a no. You should only pitch investors who can say yes. Every investor you don't disqualify costs you hours of research, intro work, meeting prep, and follow-up. These hard filters shave weeks off what is an exhausting experience.

There are five hard filters.

One. Thesis fit. At least twenty percent of their portfolio should be in your sector. If they don't already invest in your space, they'd have to learn an entire market to back you. Most won't.

Two. Stage fit. At least fifteen percent of their deals should be at your stage. A stage-mismatched meeting happens for the investor's benchmarking, not yours. They aren't going to write the check.

Three. Geography. At least five percent of their deals in your market. Investors are far more comfortable deploying capital into markets they understand well, including the culture, the regulatory environment, and the competitive landscape.

Four. Portfolio conflicts. No direct competitors in their portfolio. Adjacent is fine. Direct means same customer, same problem, same business model. Investors almost never bet against a company they already own.

Five. Investment velocity. At least one deal closed in the last six months. Funds that have gone quiet are often out of capital, even if they say otherwise. They'll take meetings to look active and never write a check.

These percentages are bare minimums. Higher percentages should give you more confidence in the investor.

Five filters. All binary. Pass or remove. Once you've run every investor through these, you've cut thousands down to a few hundred. The next step is tiering what's left with soft filters.
